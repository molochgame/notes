## Intelligence

Title ideas: "How is AI doing any of this?" "We finally understand intelligence"

* https://gwern.net/scaling-hypothesis "bag of tricks"

I think we understand intelligence well enough now. We're going to cover what it even means to "think".
Breaking down problems into sub problems, which can then be combined to accomplish novel things, with a pattern matching device to apply the right trick at the right time., teaching the fundamentals of algebra lets you solve any algebra problem, alpha go, AI mathematics research, leetcode patterns, etc. Example: Once you learn to drive to the store, you can probably navigate the car anywhere. Intelligence is like a Turing completeness for solving problems, and we now have AI systems that can do them all. 

We can break them down and discuss them. You could try to classify them: Memory (can you store newly acquired information) Reflection (can you accurately assess whether you are making progress toward your goal, or stuck and need to attempt something new) and we could go on, but in fact these blend together and classifying them detracts from the central concept: these are all just a learned set of "moves", like in a boardgame. If you can pattern match from experience well enough to apply the right trick at the right time you can solve the problem.

What if the problem requires a new trick? All tricks are merely combinations of smaller "tricks".
solve 2x - 3 = 5
If you don't know algebra, you might apply a combination of the Bruteforce trick and Reflection: try random numbers as x, 
If you learned algebra, you know several tricks: solve for x, which is itself a set of tricks like (subtract/add from both sides, multiply/divide both sides)
If I come and give you an algebraic problem you never learned the trick for, you will be back to brute force. For example:
x^x = 27
Try solving for x. You almost certainly cant. The answer is of course 3, but how do you solve it algebraicly?

I independently discovered this in high school, and used it to stump some math teachers. I'm not the first person to discover it of course, but I did solve it independently as well, by using a combination of existing tricks you already know:
solve easier versions of a problem first

keep trying different things

For example, we could change the 27 to a 4, but then the x is just different. We still can't solve for it.

x + x = 10
x * x = 10
x ^ x = 10

Eventually you figure out that all algebraic operators you know are on an infinite table, where you can start withs something like addition, each one down the repeated version of the last one, and where the inverse function is just kind of like "do whatever you have to do" to get the answer. In the same way you have to do a bunch of long division operations to find the inverse of multiplication, things just get worse as you go down. Even at square root, a bunch of techniques are just fancy versions of brute force.
